# AthletiQ — Market & Competitive Analysis

**Project:** AthletiQ — AI-Powered Digital Sports Scientist  
**Problem Statement:** The Digital Sports Scientist: Bringing Elite Performance Analysis to Every Grassroots Coach  
**Context:** Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (MYAS) / Sports Authority of India (SAI)  
**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft / Hackathon  
**Date:** August 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary

AthletiQ operates in the intersection of sports technology, computer vision, video analysis, and accessible sports science.

The underlying problem is real: coaches can capture large amounts of training footage on smartphones, but converting that footage into consistent, objective, longitudinal performance data is more difficult. Professional biomechanics laboratories can provide sophisticated measurements, but they require specialized equipment, controlled environments, and expert personnel.

However, the market is **not empty**. Existing products already provide significant portions of the proposed functionality.

Examples include:

- **CoachNow Analyze**, which provides AI-enabled skeleton tracking, joint-angle measurements, video comparison, annotations, slow motion, and cloud-based athlete content management. [1][2]
- **Onform**, which provides AI skeleton tracking, joint-angle analysis, side-by-side and overlay comparison, multi-angle capture, and sports video-analysis workflows. [3][4]
- **AXIFY**, an on-device sports-video analysis application that advertises joint-angle measurement and frame-by-frame biomechanical feedback without requiring cloud upload. [5]
- **Traeceet**, which provides AI pose tracking, joint-angle measurement, movement tracing, and biomechanical overlays. [6]

Therefore, AthletiQ should **not** compete simply on the claim that it can perform pose estimation or calculate joint angles.

The proposed competitive position is:

> **AthletiQ is a smartphone-first, grassroots-focused Digital Sports Scientist that combines movement measurement with athlete-specific baselines, longitudinal monitoring, deviation detection, and academy/federation-scale athlete management.**

The strongest differentiation is the **combination of affordability, personalization, longitudinal monitoring, grassroots deployment, and scalable institutional architecture**.

# 2. Market Problem

## 2.1 The Existing Sports-Science Gap

At one end of the market are professional sports-science environments with:

- Motion-capture systems.
- Specialized cameras.
- Force plates.
- Wearables.
- Sports scientists.
- Biomechanical laboratories.
- Controlled testing environments.

At the other end are grassroots coaches who may primarily have:

- Smartphones.
- Training videos.
- Manual observation.
- Basic timing tools.
- Spreadsheets or notebooks.

This creates a gap:

```text
Professional Sports Science
        │
        │ High cost
        │ Specialized equipment
        │ Specialized expertise
        ▼
--------------------------------
       ACCESSIBILITY GAP
--------------------------------
        ▲
        │ Smartphone footage
        │ Manual assessment
        │ Limited analytics
        │
Grassroots Coaches
```

AthletiQ targets this accessibility gap.

# 4. Existing Competitive Landscape

The market can be divided into four broad categories.

## Category A — AI Sports Video Analysis

Examples:

- CoachNow
- Onform
- AXIFY
- Traeceet

These products use video, computer vision, or AI to assist movement/technique analysis.

## Category B — General Sports Video Platforms

Examples:

- Hudl and similar video-analysis platforms.

These emphasize video management, coaching workflows, team analysis, and performance review.

## Category C — Professional Biomechanics Systems

These include laboratory and specialized motion-capture solutions.

They provide much deeper measurement capabilities but generally require more expensive infrastructure and expertise.

## Category D — Manual Smartphone Analysis

Many coaches still use:

- Phone camera.
- Slow-motion playback.
- Video annotations.
- Manual measurements.
- Spreadsheets.

This is inexpensive but highly dependent on coach time and expertise.

# 5. Competitor 1 — CoachNow

## Overview

CoachNow is a sports coaching and video-analysis platform. Its current Analyze offering includes AI-enabled skeleton tracking where users can overlay a skeleton and inspect joint angles. It also supports side-by-side video comparison, overlays, annotations, slow-motion playback, and cloud content management. [1][2]

### Key capabilities

- AI-enabled skeleton tracking.
- Joint-angle measurements.
- Side-by-side comparison.
- Video overlays.
- Slow-motion playback.
- Annotation tools.
- Voice-over feedback.
- Cloud library.
- Athlete/coach collaboration.

CoachNow explicitly positions its video analysis as a coaching-support tool rather than a replacement for coaching expertise. [2]

### Strengths

- Mature coaching workflow.
- Strong video-analysis functionality.
- AI skeleton tracking.
- Joint-angle measurement.
- Video comparison.
- Multi-sport positioning.
- Cloud-based athlete content management.

### Limitations relative to AthletiQ's proposed positioning

The major opportunity is not to claim that CoachNow lacks these capabilities. Instead, AthletiQ can differentiate by making **personalized longitudinal biomechanical monitoring and grassroots institutional deployment** central to the product.

### Competitive threat

**Very High**

CoachNow demonstrates that smartphone-based AI movement analysis is already commercially viable.

---

# 6. Competitor 2 — Onform

## Overview

Onform is a sports video-analysis platform with tools including skeleton tracking, angles, side-by-side comparison, overlays, drawing, voiceover, and stopwatch functionality. Its documentation states that skeleton tracking maps body movement and allows users to inspect changing joint angles. [3]

Onform also supports multi-angle recording and sports-specific workflows. Its track-and-field offering, for example, includes multi-camera recording, technique comparison, and organization of athletes and teams. [4]

### Strengths

- Strong video-analysis workflow.
- AI skeleton tracking.
- Joint-angle analysis.
- Side-by-side comparison.
- Overlay comparison.
- Multi-camera capture.
- Athlete/team organization.
- Sports-specific features.

### Limitations relative to AthletiQ's proposed positioning

Again, the opportunity is not simply "better video analysis."

AthletiQ can differentiate by making:

- Personal athlete baselines.
- Cross-session metric tracking.
- Automated deviation detection.
- Grassroots affordability.
- Academy-wide monitoring.
- Federation-level aggregation.

core product concepts.

### Competitive threat

**Very High**

Onform is one of the closest conceptual competitors.

---

# 7. Competitor 3 — AXIFY

## Overview

AXIFY is a sports video coaching application that advertises on-device AI for measuring joint angles from video. It states that analysis can occur without requiring an account or cloud upload and positions the product around frame-by-frame biomechanical feedback. [5]

### Strengths

- On-device AI.
- Joint-angle tracking.
- No cloud upload required for its advertised workflow.
- Smartphone-first.
- Low hardware requirement.
- Direct biomechanical feedback.

### Competitive significance

AXIFY demonstrates an important point:

> **"Smartphone + AI + joint-angle analysis" is not itself a sufficient USP.**

AthletiQ therefore needs a broader product layer around the raw analysis.

### Competitive threat

**Medium to High**

**Medium**

# 9. Competitor 5 — General Video Analysis Platforms

Platforms such as Hudl and other sports-video systems focus heavily on video capture, organization, analysis, collaboration, team workflows, and coaching.

### Strengths

# 10. Professional Biomechanics Laboratories

Professional systems remain an important indirect competitor.

They may use:

- Multiple cameras.
- Motion capture.
- Force plates.
- Inertial sensors.
- Specialized analysis software.
- Sports scientists.

### Strengths

- High measurement quality.
- Rich biomechanical data.
- Professional validation.
- Advanced 3D analysis.

### Limitations

- Expensive.
- Specialized equipment.
- Specialized personnel.
- Less practical for daily grassroots training.
- More difficult to deploy across thousands of centres.

### AthletiQ's positioning

AthletiQ should **not claim to replace these systems**.

Instead:

> AthletiQ provides accessible movement intelligence for routine monitoring, while professional laboratories remain appropriate for high-precision assessment.

### Competitive threat

**Low as a direct product competitor; high as a benchmark for accuracy.**

# 13. Competitive Gap

## Gap 1 — From Video Analysis to Athlete Intelligence

Most video-analysis workflows answer:

> "What happened in this video?"

AthletiQ should answer:

> "How does this athlete's movement today compare with their own historical pattern?"

This is a fundamental product distinction.

---

## Gap 2 — From Single Session to Longitudinal Monitoring

Instead of:

```text
Video → Analysis → Feedback
```

AthletiQ should build:

```text
Session 1
   ↓
Session 2
   ↓
Session 3
   ↓
Personal Baseline
   ↓
Session 4
   ↓
Deviation / Trend
```

---

## Gap 3 — From Individual Coach to Academy

The system should not stop at a coach's phone.

It should eventually support:

```text
Coach
 ↓
Athlete Records
 ↓
Academy Dashboard
 ↓
Multiple Coaches
 ↓
Multiple Centres
```

---

## Gap 4 — From Academy to Federation

A major long-term opportunity is standardized monitoring.

```text
Centre A ─┐
Centre B ─┤
Centre C ─┼──→ Standardized Data → Federation
Centre D ─┤
Centre E ─┘
```

This could allow authorized organizations to monitor athlete development across geographically distributed centres.

---

## Gap 5 — Grassroots-First Design

Many existing products serve a broad coaching market.

AthletiQ can be explicitly designed around grassroots constraints:

- Smartphone-first.
- Simple workflows.
- Low hardware requirements.
- Low-bandwidth operation.
- Offline/edge inference where feasible.
- Minimal technical expertise required.
- Centralized but role-controlled records.
- Standardized protocols.

# 14. AthletiQ's Proposed USP

### Primary USP

> **A smartphone-first Digital Sports Scientist that creates personal movement baselines and tracks objective performance trends across training sessions for grassroots athletes.**

### Secondary USPs

#### 1. Personal Baseline

Athletes are primarily compared with their own historical performance.

#### 2. Longitudinal Intelligence

The product is designed around repeated sessions rather than isolated videos.

#### 3. Grassroots Accessibility

Designed for coaches who may not have access to laboratories or sports scientists.

#### 4. Human-in-the-Loop

AI measures and flags; the coach interprets and decides.

#### 5. Scalable Architecture

Can progress from individual athlete → academy → federation.

# 15. SWOT Analysis

## Strengths

- Smartphone-first.
- Low hardware requirements.
- Computer-vision based automation.
- Personal baseline concept.
- Longitudinal tracking.
- Coach decision-support model.
- Potential for academy/federation scaling.
- Modular multi-sport architecture.

## Weaknesses

- 2D video has inherent limitations.
- Camera placement affects measurements.
- Occlusion can reduce pose accuracy.
- Lighting and clothing affect detection.
- Smartphone cameras vary significantly.
- Requires strong validation before high-stakes use.
- Initial MVP will support only a limited number of sports/movements.

## Opportunities

- Grassroots sports academies.
- Schools and universities.
- State sports programs.
- SAI-related centres.
- Federations.
- Athlete development programs.
- Sports-science education.
- Integration with wearables.
- Integration with professional sports scientists.

## Threats

- Established video-analysis platforms.
- Rapid improvement of competing AI tools.
- Proprietary biomechanics systems.
- Privacy concerns.
- User distrust of AI measurements.
- Overclaiming accuracy.
- Regulatory/data-protection requirements.

# 16. Porter's Five Forces — Simplified

## Competitive Rivalry — High

Sports video analysis already has multiple established products.

**Implication:** AthletiQ must differentiate beyond basic video analysis.

## Threat of New Entrants — High

Pose-estimation APIs and open-source models reduce technical barriers.

**Implication:** The defensible layer should be product workflow, longitudinal data, validation, domain expertise, and deployment ecosystem.

## Supplier Power — Medium

AthletiQ may depend on:

- AI model providers.
- Cloud infrastructure.
- GPU resources.
- Storage providers.

Open-source models can reduce dependency.

## Buyer Power — Medium to High

Coaches and academies may be price-sensitive.

**Implication:** MVP must demonstrate clear time savings and useful insights.

## Threat of Substitutes — High

Substitutes include:

- Manual video review.
- Sports scientists.
- Wearables.
- Motion-capture labs.
- Existing coaching platforms.

**Implication:** AthletiQ must complement existing coaching practices rather than simply replace them.

# 17. Defensibility Strategy

Pose estimation itself is not a strong moat because high-quality models are increasingly accessible.

AthletiQ's defensibility should come from:

### 1. Validated Metrics

Build reliable sport-specific measurement pipelines.

### 2. Longitudinal Athlete Data

With appropriate consent and governance, repeated sessions create valuable athlete histories.

### 3. Sport-Specific Intelligence

Develop validated movement-analysis protocols for individual sports.

### 4. Coach Workflow

Make the product extremely easy for coaches to use.

### 5. Institutional Network

Academy and federation integrations can create switching costs.

### 6. Evaluation Dataset

Build carefully governed datasets for sport-specific movement validation.

### 7. Personalization

Use athlete-specific baselines rather than one-size-fits-all thresholds.

---

# 18. Recommended Competitive Strategy

AthletiQ should **not** attempt to beat established platforms on every feature.

### Avoid positioning:

> "We are another AI video analysis application."

### Prefer:

> "Existing sports-video platforms help coaches review footage. AthletiQ adds a longitudinal sports-science layer that turns repeated smartphone footage into measurable athlete baselines, trends, and coach-review flags."

This is a much more defensible hackathon narrative.

---

# 19. MVP Competitive Strategy

The MVP should prove one thing extremely well:

> **Can ordinary smartphone footage be converted into useful, repeatable movement metrics that help a grassroots coach monitor an athlete over time?**

### MVP flow

```text
Smartphone Video
       ↓
Pose Estimation
       ↓
Movement Metrics
       ↓
Session Record
       ↓
3–5 Valid Sessions
       ↓
Personal Baseline
       ↓
New Session
       ↓
Deviation
       ↓
Coach Review
```

Do not try to build a full competitor to CoachNow or Onform in the first version.

---

# 20. Recommended MVP Differentiation

For the hackathon, focus on these five capabilities:

### 1. Smartphone Capture

No specialized hardware.

### 2. Reliable Pose Analysis

Show the skeleton and confidence.

### 3. Quantitative Metrics

Show actual numbers.

### 4. Personal Baseline

Show the athlete's historical normal range.

### 5. Coach Insight

Convert the numbers into a useful review recommendation.

Example:

```text
ATHLETE: Rahul
SESSION: 18

Metric              Baseline    Current
────────────────────────────────────────
Knee Angle           91°         94°
Stride Symmetry      96%         89%
Cadence              176         174
Movement Variability  4.2%        7.1%

⚠ REVIEW RECOMMENDED

Stride symmetry is lower than the
athlete's recent baseline.

Review the current footage for
technique changes.
```

# 21. Strategic Positioning Statement

> **AthletiQ is not a replacement for professional biomechanics laboratories, sports scientists, or coaches. It is an accessible digital sports-science layer that enables routine movement monitoring using smartphone video.**

This distinction is important for both credibility and responsible AI.

# 22. Competitive Conclusion

The competitive analysis shows that the basic technology behind AthletiQ is **already commercially available in several forms**.

CoachNow and Onform already demonstrate smartphone-based AI skeleton tracking and joint-angle analysis. AXIFY and Traeceet further demonstrate that on-device or smartphone-based biomechanical analysis is becoming increasingly accessible. [1][3][5][6]

Therefore:

> **Pose estimation + joint angles + video analysis should not be presented as AthletiQ's primary innovation.**

The opportunity is to combine these capabilities into a system specifically designed for:

```text
Grassroots Accessibility
        +
Personal Athlete Baseline
        +
Longitudinal Monitoring
        +
Automated Deviation Detection
        +
Coach Decision Support
        +
Academy Management
        +
Federation Scalability
```

### Final competitive proposition

> **"From smartphone video to personal sports-science intelligence — built for the grassroots athlete."**
